## Outcome

Implemented an independent wav2vec2 forced aligner and evaluated it against the approved 16 kHz corpus. It runs on CPU through ONNX Runtime and returns word boundaries plus a provider-specific mean emission log-probability. This confirms the path functions; the oracle timing accuracy is not yet strong enough to treat timestamps as precise censorship boundaries without further evaluation.

Start errors are the more meaningful comparison because SAPI ends are approximate. Start error was largest in this small corpus for clean speech (398 ms), repeated targets (376 ms), and rapid speech (359 ms); speech-noise (139 ms) and quiet speech (181 ms) were lower. End errors were high in every category and should not be read as precise phonetic-boundary accuracy. Per-category values and clips are in `results\wav2vec2-aligner\analysis.json`.

The final recursive rescan found **1,177** usable ASR raw JSON observations (including cold/warm suffixes and nested whisper.cpp configurations) and emitted 1,177 per-observation alignments. Macro-average per-observation start/end error was 254/502 ms; 23 hypothesized words had null alignment. Native word timestamps were present in 434 observations. On that identical observation subset, aligner start/end errors were 252/499 ms versus native 237/357 ms. Matching used exact-word Levenshtein pairs, so substitutions/insertions are not incorrectly timed. Native/aligner paired observations are also in `analysis.json`.

Empty text on silence returns no words and no missing alignments. Forcing `banana` onto silence still returns an apparent interval, with word score -5.02; non-speech forced words scored -5.29 and -4.73. On clean speech, eight true words averaged -0.106 versus -14.086 for two appended hallucinated words (13.98 log-probability difference). This is a small illustrative probe only: forced alignment does not itself reject hallucinations, and scores are uncalibrated, not a universal threshold. Unsupported alphabet words are preserved with null times and counted missing.

A 23.5 s repeated-clip smoke test exercised chunking (42 words, 0 missing). It is not an accuracy validation for naturally long/uneven speech; proportional transcript partitioning can misplace chunks when speech density is highly nonuniform.

## Corpus v2

### Exact-boundary oracle

Evaluated all 30 `set=timing` clips in the frozen corpus using the exact sample-index boundaries (94 matched word instances, 0 aligner-missing words). The earlier corpus-v1 oracle figures above are withdrawn.

| Boundary | Mean absolute | Median absolute | p95 absolute | Signed bias | Within 50 / 100 / 200 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Start | 82.0 ms | 80.2 ms | 158.5 ms | +67.1 ms | 29.8% / 68.1% / 96.8% |
| End | 147.1 ms | 141.4 ms | 227.8 ms | −147.1 ms | 1.1% / 16.0% / 88.3% |

All category metrics (mean, median, p95, signed bias, and thresholds), voice metrics, and category×voice metrics are in [`oracle_v2.json`](C:\scratch\asr50\results_v2\wav2vec2-aligner\oracle_v2.json). By voice, Zira start/end mean absolute error was 51.5/126.3 ms (median 25.6/112.1; p95 95.0/206.4); David was 112.4/167.8 ms (median 108.2/166.5; p95 181.7/227.8). Starts were most degraded for pink noise at −5 dB SNR (203 ms mean, p95 551); its end mean was 294 ms, p95 510. Most other categories had mean starts 51–110 ms and mean ends 102–183 ms.

The signed biases are systematic and asymmetric. The aligner maps the first/last nonblank character frames to frame-cell edges (`first_frame / frame_rate`, `(last_frame + 1) / frame_rate`); at 50 frames/s, center-versus-edge convention can contribute about 10 ms, far below the observed +67 ms start and −147 ms end bias. CTC often needs blank frames between words: this implementation excludes those blanks from word spans, tending to delay starts and end words before the hard trimmed end. A boundary-specific diagnostic correction of −67 ms to starts and +147 ms to ends is worth documenting/testing, but should **not** be applied as a universal calibration: David and Zira differ substantially, noisy pink −5 dB is anomalous, and calibration must be checked on held-out voices/categories. No correction was applied.

### ASR pipeline and score separation

After about 21 minutes of polling (outputs stable across the final poll), the final recursive scan found 720 usable timing-clip ASR observations across 30 unique clips and 22 runner/config combinations. The `ort-whisper-small-en` CPU/DML directories contributed 60 records each (multiple per-clip runs); `policy-off` had no usable timing-clip records and was excluded. 119 aligner word timings were missing across these ASR transcripts.

For every ASR transcript, `matched_word_pairs` supplied exact lexical matches before timing scoring. There were 472 observations with native timestamps. On this same set of ASR output observations, each method's own matched-word pairs yielded 1,167 finite boundary pairs; aligner versus native mean absolute errors were **70.5 vs 91.6 ms starts** and **130.9 vs 169.3 ms ends** (native start/end signed bias +62.1/−96.0 ms). Across all 720 observations the aligner boundary means were 71.4/132.1 ms; these are corpus-specific and include repeated runner configurations.

Across correctly matched ASR words, mean aligner word score was −0.769 (n=1,681), versus −6.729 for inserted/substituted words (n=839); AUROC with higher scores favoring correct lexical matches was 0.934. This supports investigating the D67-style provider-specific score as a quality signal, not a calibrated confidence threshold.
