# WP15 — Corpus v2 reproducibility diagnosis

## Result

The whole-file hash mismatch was **not nondeterministic SAPI synthesis or audio
processing**. The first differing layer was WAV serialization: libsndfile's
float WAV `PEAK` metadata chunk embeds the current Unix timestamp. For example,
the PEAK timestamp changed from `1795741813` to `1795741888`; its peak value,
chunk layout and all `data` PCM bytes were identical. The sample audio was
reproducible; the WAV container bytes were not.

Diagnostics from a cache-backed `regen_a` / `regen_b` pair:

| Layer | Equality |
|---|---:|
| Content-addressed SAPI raw WAVs and metadata | 22/22 |
| Trimming/resampling, speech-only, noise/effects, mix arrays and WAV diagnostics | 224/224 |
| Constructed speech-only WAVs | 30/30 |
| Final audio files | 60/60 |

The comparator reports `ALL IDENTICAL`. Frozen-vs-regen comparison found
**60/60 PCM `data` chunks identical** but **0/60 entire files byte-identical**.
Inspection showed the same PEAK timestamp variation. Do not replace the frozen
artifact: evaluators retain it and its existing manifest.

**Summary (<200 words):** The original all-clip SHA-256 mismatch came from
libsndfile's wall-clock timestamp in the WAV `PEAK` chunk, not SAPI, NumPy
seeds, Python ordering, or resampling. All PCM data in the frozen corpus is
bit-identical to cache-backed regeneration. The generator now uses a
content-addressed SAPI WAV cache and zeros the non-audio PEAK timestamp;
two builds into `regen_a` and `regen_b` compare identical across 22 SAPI inputs,
224 intermediate layers, 30 speech-only tracks, and all 60 final WAVs. Frozen
corpus and its manifest remain untouched and remain the evaluation artifact.
