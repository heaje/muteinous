**Status:** complete (final update 2026-11-27). ## 1. Labels

Conventions follow `C:\code\hushpatch\scripts\eval_context.py`: 1 = bad language (swearing, vulgar insult/slur, or deity name as an exclamation/oath); 0 = literal/innocent, sincere religious or mythological reference, or name/title. “Screamin' bloody murder” is innocent. The reviewed `screw` idiom convention treats dismissive uses as censurable but hardware and innocent idioms such as “screw up the courage” as keep. Quoted/reported swearing is labeled by the word's use, not whether the speaker endorses it. These are benchmark conventions, not universal judgments.

| Set | Cases | Censor / keep | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| New English | 150 | 75 / 75 | Exactly 30 clear positives, 30 clear negatives, 90 contrasts; profanity, idioms, religion, invented names/places, quotes/reports, homonyms |
| Hushpatch SYNTHETIC | 15 | 3 / 12 | Extracted only to WP23 scratch; REAL subtitle IDs were not used |
| Exploratory multilingual | 60 | 30 / 30 | 20 each Spanish, French, German; non-native, low-confidence labels |

Ground-truth JSON has sentence, target word, zero-based start/end character offsets (end exclusive), category, and integer label. Authored text is in `spike/asr50/context_model/make_set.py`; all generated data is under `C:\scratch\asr50\WP23\labels\`. Eleven edge-label notes are in `labels\label_uncertainties.json`.

Audit against Hushpatch's word list: 126/150 authored target terms match a listed word/form, and 109 have the list's `ambiguous` flag. The remaining 24 are clear controls outside the current list; they are not represented as production candidates. The context measurements call raw Phi only, without Hushpatch's separate name/innocent/idiom/interjection rules.
