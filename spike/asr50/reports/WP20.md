# WP20 — Boundary sensitivity and calibration

## Scope and method

Read-only analysis of frozen corpus v2 and existing results. The audio source was `C:\scratch\asr50\WP15\speech_only16k`; references and word intervals came from `C:\scratch\asr50\corpus_v2_frozen\ground_truth_v2.json`. Per word, calculated a centered 5 ms moving RMS envelope, restricted to the constructed interval, then found the first/last samples at or above the word-peak threshold. Offsets below are perceptual edge minus constructed edge; negative end means the proposed endpoint is earlier. Results are sample-grid/envelope estimates, not independently human-annotated truth. The script is `C:\scratch\asr50\WP20\analysis.py`.

## Conclusions and summary (<250 words)

Boundary convention is consequential: the oracle's trim-edge end MAE/bias of 147.1/−147.1 ms becomes 47.9/−35.1 ms at −20 dB, while several native Whisper.cpp and Parakeet end errors instead increase at −20 dB. Thus the broad claim that endpoints are systematically worse than starts does **not** survive all conventions. The trim-edge aligner start/end signed biases (+67.1/−147.1 ms) also shrink/change materially. Some relative patterns survive: David's envelope edge offsets are larger than Zira's; on trim-edge truth the aligner remains voice-dependent, and single offsets often fail to improve the other voice (notably Zira-trained correction tested on David raises start MAE 51.5→79.1 ms). Category-trained aligner offsets reduce trim-edge MAE in both held-out directions, but not uniformly at other thresholds. Parakeet calibration is convention-sensitive: clean+noise-trained end offsets often lower the other-category end MAE, whereas voice-held-out improvements vary and some start MAEs worsen. No calibration was written to results.

On 94 constructed words, envelope alternatives move boundaries inward by mean +42 to +73 ms at starts and −56 to −112 ms at ends. Re-scoring against those conventions materially changes apparent errors and can reverse conclusions, so “timing accuracy” here is boundary-definition-specific, not a single convention-independent ranking. The trim baseline reproduces WP14 oracle and WP9 Parakeet values exactly. Matched-pair denominators vary with hypotheses/configurations; unaligned reference words are excluded as in `score.py::matched_word_pairs`. Read-only analysis used local Python 3.12 and existing WAV/JSON files; no downloads/installs; no result files or timings were altered.
